# The Ship

**Source:** https://lethal-company.fandom.com/wiki/The_Ship

## Overview

The Autopilot Ship, designated **FORTUNE-9**, is where every crew begins a contract in *Lethal Company*. It is simultaneously the only transport between moons and the crew's base of operations: a single room holding the terminal, monitors, a suit rack, furnishings, and stored scrap and equipment. This page covers autopilot and departure rules, every stock component, purchasable ship upgrades, and the important rules governing entities that can get inside.

## Layout

At one end sits the **Terminal**, an array of **monitors**, and furnishings. At the other end is the **main pressure door** leading outside. The exterior has a railing with two access ladders to the ground and a third ladder to the roof.

## Autopilot and Departure

Confirming a destination in the terminal, then pulling the lever in front of the main monitor, lands the ship. **The ship lands at 8:00 a.m. and begins departing at 11:57 p.m.** Automatic departure can be overridden early three ways:

- **Disengaging the brake lever** — the ship leaves regardless of who is aboard. Anyone left behind is marked *missing*, functionally identical to death.
- **Voting to leave** — deceased crew members can vote with [RMB]. A unanimous vote triggers an autopilot warning announcing the departure time, attributed to "hazardous conditions."
- **Total crew death** — the ship departs automatically. **Equipment, ship upgrades, decor, and credits are retained, but all stored scrap is lost**, which can be an irrecoverable setback. The game attributes this to "loss of communication with crew."

### The "on the ship" rule

Being "on the ship" is defined very loosely: **anywhere on the ship counts, including standing on the railing.** The game does not mark you missing until the ship doors close — so in an emergency you can sprint, jetpack, or ride a Tulip Snake onto the ship at the last second and survive.

## Stock Components

### Terminal

The most important component. It sets the destination moon, purchases equipment/upgrades/decor from the Store, displays scanned entity information, runs `SCAN` to count scrap on the planet, opens and closes secure doors, and temporarily disables turrets and landmines. `VIEW MONITOR` lets one person run camera duty from the terminal itself. The `OTHER` command reveals SCAN, VIEW MONITOR, FLASH, PING, and TRANSMIT, while EJECT, SIGURD, SWITCH, and other secret codes remain hidden from the listings.

### Monitors

The **main monitor** shows the local map around a selected crew member. The **right monitor** shows CCTV of the area outside the pressure door. The **pair of monitors at the top** display the profit quota and deadline.

### Light switch

Toggles the ship's interior lighting. Some light sources stay lit regardless — cozy lights, the Apparatus, a Fancy Lamp, and the glow from the teleporter and inverse teleporter. **Placing the light switch beneath one of these makes it findable when the ship goes dark**, such as after a lightning strike. The Remote scrap item duplicates the switch's function.

### Main pressure door

Two buttons, open and close, at the rear of the ship. **The door can only stay closed while powered, and it loses power over about 30 seconds**; a display shows remaining pressure, and at 0% the door opens on its own. Refilling takes about 6 seconds. Another CCTV monitor sits above the panel.

The door blocks entities such as Eyeless Dogs and the Masked — **with one exception: the Giant Sapsucker can pry the door open**, which forces it open automatically.

### Suit rack

Changes suit color. Holds the stock Decoy Suit plus any purchased: Green (60 credits), Purple (70), Hazard (90), Bee (110), Bunny (200), and Pajama (900).

### Stock furnishings

Movable with [B] and hideable with [X]; `STORAGE` in the terminal lists everything hidden. The ship ships with a **Cupboard** (stores small scrap and equipment), a **File Cabinet**, and **Bunkbeds**.

### Magnet

Used to hoist up the **Company Cruiser**, toggled by a lever on the front right of the ship. Switching it on or off can briefly pull employees in as well — attributed in-fiction to the metal screws on the employee suit mask.

## Ship Upgrades

Purchased through the terminal; these cannot leave the ship.

- **Loud Horn (100 credits)** — a foghorn audible everywhere including inside the facility, activated by a pull cord. Guides lost crew home, signals events like deaths or the time, and draws outdoor creatures such as Eyeless Dogs toward the ship's front.
- **Teleporter (375 credits)** — teleports whichever employee is being viewed on the monitor back to the ship. **All scrap and equipment they carry is dropped at their location.** Works on employees who are alive, dead, or Masked, as long as the body is intact. **10-second cooldown.**
- **Inverse Teleporter (425 credits)** — teleports any number of employees standing on its pad to a random location inside the facility. Their carried items are dropped on the ship first. **5-minute cooldown.**
- **Signal Translator (255 credits)** — lets someone at the terminal broadcast a nine-character message (the game claims ten) using `TRANSMIT [message]`.

## Additional Furnishings

Purchasable decor rotates per quota and includes Cozy Lights, Goldfish, Jack O' Lantern, Television, Record Player, Romantic Table, Shower, Table, Toilet, Welcome Mat, and Plushie Pajama Man.

## Important Notes and Warnings

- **Furniture placed in the ship provides "quota luck"**, reducing the quota multiplier. See Profit Quota for per-item values.
- **Furniture has collision with players but NOT with entities.** Forts or walls built from furniture offer zero protection. It can still be used to climb above entities that can't reach you — for example glitching on top of the Cupboard.
- Furniture cannot be moved while the ship is arriving or departing.
- Any entity on the ship clips through it when the ship leaves a moon.
- **After the ship leaves and the main door closes automatically, employees on the catwalks or roof are teleported inside.** This can be exploited: if an Eyeless Dog or Circuit Bees have gotten into the ship, wait outside on the roof through autopilot departure to escape safely rather than entering and dying.

### Entities that can enter through an open main door

- **Masked** — by following you or another player, or by being teleported in. They will not spawn on moons that spawn neither Masked nor mask-related items.
- **Eyeless Dogs** — enter on hearing any noise, and are among the most dangerous outdoor entities. A single dog hearing a noise inside the ship while the doors are open can annihilate a team. **If the noise is continuous — a Disco Ball or Record Player — or the dog keeps bumping a noisemaker such as a Whoopie-Cushion or Soccer Ball, it can stay indefinitely**, making entering the ship a death sentence. Turn off music players and keep noisemakers out of reach when a dog is near.
- **Ghost Girl** — can enter while haunting an employee inside. Only the haunted player sees her directly; others can see her on the ship's CCTV.
- **Circuit Bees** — enter while trying to reclaim their Bee Hive.
