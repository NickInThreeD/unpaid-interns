# Map Hazard

**Source:** https://lethal-company.fandom.com/wiki/Map_Hazard

## Overview

Map Hazards are the static environmental dangers scattered through facilities in *Lethal Company*. Unlike entities, they do not move or hunt — they simply occupy space, and they are frequently fatal. Three exist: **Landmines**, **Turrets**, and **Spike Traps**. This page is the summary index; each has a dedicated page with full behavioral detail.

## Key Point

**All map hazards can be temporarily deactivated from the ship's terminal** by keying in the two-digit codes shown on the radar map. This makes a crew member on camera duty a direct counter to every hazard type.

## Landmines

Explosive devices scattered randomly on the facility floor. They occasionally emit a distinctive beep that reveals their presence. **A landmine only triggers when an item is dropped on it, or when a player (or player's body) moves *off* it** — not when weight is first applied. This delay is what makes the following counterplay possible:

1. Throw a flashbang or Easter Egg at it.
2. Drop a player's body on it and run away.
3. Leave a body near it and let a Hoarding Bug carry it over the mine.
4. Step on it and have someone teleport you off.
5. Die next to it and hope your body ragdolls onto it — which can also kill whatever killed you.

**Count per moon: 0–35.**

## Turrets

Mechanical devices that fire rapidly at any player they see. **Turrets are the only hazard that does not kill in one hit**, though they can still kill within seconds. On detection they emit an orange light and an audio cue and track the player's last known position, only disengaging when the player leaves line of sight — so simply backtracking out of the room is usually enough to survive.

**Hitting a turret with a melee item makes it spin while firing for a short period** (berserk mode).

**Count per moon: 0–35** (per this page; the dedicated Turret page lists moon-specific maxima).

## Spike Traps

A spiked platform suspended from a large support bar that slams down. Two activation behaviors:

- **Timer mode** — slams at a randomly chosen interval (roughly 0.8–26.2 seconds). Easier to survive, because the slam sound advertises the trap's presence.
- **Detection mode** — slams only when a player is beneath it. Much harder to avoid, since there is no warning; you must watch where you are going.

**Count per moon: 0–18.**
