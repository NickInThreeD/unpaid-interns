# The Company (71-Gordion)

**Source:** https://lethal-company.fandom.com/wiki/The_Company

## Overview

71-Gordion is the destination housing The Company — the corporate entity that employs the crew and buys their scrap. Rated hazard level "Safe", it is a landless, permanently stormy moon where the ship lands on a loading dock in front of a colossal walled structure. Gordion is where scrap is converted into credits and profit quotas are fulfilled, and it is the only moon where the clock does not run.

## Key Points

- **Cost:** 0 credits. **Risk level:** Safe.
- **Time does not progress on Gordion** — employees can land and take off as often as they like without consuming quota days. This makes it a safe place to buy from the Store without wasting mission time.
- Selling is done by placing scrap on the desk and **ringing the bell**.
- Terminal entry lists population, fauna, and status as "Unknown", with conditions of "No land masses. Continual Storms."

## The Company's Objective

The Company collects scrap from abandoned, formerly industrialized moons for unstated purposes. It motivates employees two ways:

1. **Credits for scrap**, which fund access to richer moons, Store equipment, and ship upgrades and decorations.
2. **Discharge on failure** — crews who miss the profit quota are fired (jettisoned into deep space).

## Selling Scrap

Scrap is sold by landing on Gordion at any point in the cycle without losing days. However, the buy rate rises sharply as the deadline approaches, so waiting is normally correct:

- **Day 1:** 30%
- **Day 2:** 53%
- **Day 3:** 77%
- **Day 4 (deadline):** 100%

Two additional rates exist but are effectively unreachable. A **123%** rate is briefly visible on the terminal if you check immediately after missing the quota deadline — but it is impossible to reach Gordion at that point, as it only appears just before the firing cutscene. A **120%** rate is referenced in one of Sigurd's logs and is not present in the game.

## The Company Monster

The Company Monster is the creature that takes the sold scrap and is presumed to operate the speaker delivering the encouraging voice lines. Employees place scrap on the desk and ring the bell to have it accept the goods in exchange for credits. **Ringing the bell excessively or making too much noise nearby can agitate the monster and result in death.**

### Voice lines

Selling triggers one of five default lines — variations on "Your work keeps The Company happy", "We value your commitment", "Your hard work is invaluable to The Company", "Your honest work is invaluable to The Company", and "You are true professionals". There is a **3% chance** a rare line replaces the default; these are noticeably distorted and unsettling, including "We need you… *glitching*", "The Company must stay happy — Company must —", "This wall cannot contain — *glitching*", and "Keep our *investors* happy."

## The Drill

Beneath the main platform, reachable only with careful jumps, sits a large metal cylinder widely assumed to be a drill. It rests on a track aimed directly at the Company building, and a green spray-painted circle marks the wall where it would strike — leading to speculation that it is the intended end of the game. It has slots for a button and two Apparatuses, but **neither can currently be connected**: the main cable from the button to the drill is severed with no way to reconnect it. Version 70 added an openable hatch at its rear. Graffiti on the drill reads "DONT TELL" and "BATTERY".

## Trivia and Special Interactions

- Putting on a **Dramatic Mask at Gordion kills the employee outright** rather than transforming them into a Masked. Using a Company Cruiser can bypass this and spawn a Masked anyway.
- In real history, Gordion was the capital of ancient Phrygia and the seat of King Midas, whose touch turned things to gold — tying into the lore's mentions of the golden planet.
- Sigurd's logs begin on Aug 22, 1968 while the terminal date reads July 3, 2532, implying The Company has operated for at least **563 years, 10 months, and 11 days**.
- Flying far enough right with a Jetpack reveals floating player models and what appears to be an early version of the selling window; neither is functional.
- The top of the Company building has collision that doesn't match its model, letting players appear to float on an invisible box.
- The Company's name may be **Halden Electronics**, which appears on both the ship manual and the cruiser manual — though this could be a subsidiary or an unrelated company.
