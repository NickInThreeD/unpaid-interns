# Guide: Camera Duty

**Source:** https://lethal-company.fandom.com/wiki/Guide:Camera_duty

## Overview

Camera duty — commonly called "ship duty" — is the team role where one employee stays aboard the autopilot ship to support the rest of the crew. The operator watches the monitors, uses the terminal to toggle doors and hazards the field team cannot reach, and relays information over Walkie-Talkies or the Signal Translator. A skilled operator materially improves both profitability and survival odds. This guide covers the role's tasks, radar interpretation, and entity identification by dot behavior.

## Tasks

A proficient camera operator can:

- Locate turrets, landmines, and creatures
- Open and close doors, and disable turrets and other hazards
- Advise employees inside the facility and communicate the time of day
- Warn of creature presence and guide the team to scrap and to the exit
- Teleport players who are in danger or already dead
- Protect Company assets if the rest of the crew perishes

Once the team exits the building and there is spare time, the operator can help ferry loot from the main entrance to shorten the return trip.

**Rotate the role early.** Letting each team member try it identifies who has the best directional and communication skills — or simply who enjoys it most.

## Operating Efficiently

### Tracking teammates

Use either the monitor buttons or the terminal's `VIEW MONITOR` and `SWITCH` commands to get an overhead view of a selected employee.

**Two speed tricks:**
- **The terminal supports copy/paste** — keep `switch` in your clipboard and paste instead of retyping.
- **Append the first three letters of a username** to `switch` to jump directly to that person.

### Spotting scrap

Scrap appears as small **pink-purple triangles**. You usually cannot tell whether it is above or below a teammate, but calling out the general direction still helps — especially where loot is clustered.

### Watching for trouble

- Track employees showing **erratic or static movement**. A corpse being dragged away (Bracken), dangling (Bunker Spider), or lying still is obvious on the monitor. Inform survivors of the loss and re-plan.
- **Teleport early.** If a teammate is surrounded or facing imminent death, do not deliberate — teleporting at the first sign of danger beats losing them. If you fail to save them, at minimum **teleport the corpse**, since an unrecovered body costs 20% of saved funds (this applies to unspent credits, not the value of retrieved scrap).
- Agree on a **signalling method** in advance: Walkie-Talkies, Signal Translator, Loud Horn, or predetermined movements. The common improvised signal is **spinning in circles** to indicate danger or request a teleport.

### Detecting a Masked impostor

Be wary of teammates with **unnatural or semi-robotic movement**: spinning when no danger exists, ignoring enemies and scrap, view and movement acting independently (perfect straight-line travel while constantly looking around), or clean direction changes with no natural stop-and-start. These suggest the player has become a **Masked**.

**Because spinning is both the standard teleport request and a Masked tell, agree on an alternative distress signal** — for example rapidly stepping left and right.

## Toggling Doors and Hazards

Viewing a coworker or Radar-Booster shows **code names above doors, turrets, landmines, and spike traps**. Typing a code toggles that object.

- **Green code** = door open, or hazard active. **Red code** = door closed, or hazard disabled.
- **Codes can be shared between objects** — disabling a turret may also shut a door, or vice versa.
- Deactivation makes a **noise**, so teammates know when it is safe to pass.
- **Doors stay toggled until changed. Turrets, landmines, and spike traps are only disabled briefly and cannot be disabled again for a short cooldown.**

### Using doors defensively

Close doors that need not be open and seal off vents where possible to make it harder for entities to reach the crew — **but take care not to block teammates' scrap routes.** Note that some entities linger by closed doors, notably **Snare Fleas and Hygroderes**.

### Power failures

- If a player **removes the Apparatus**, all facility power shuts off: doors are permanently open and uncloseable, though **turrets, landmines, and spike traps can still be disabled**.
- If **only some door codes are greyed out** and the Apparatus has not been pulled, the **Breaker Box** needs attention. It appears on the radar cams as a **lightning bolt in a circle**.

## Reading the Radar

Enemies appear as **red dots**. **Do not confuse them with thin red lines — those are vents** that entities spawn from. Dot **size and behavior** together identify the creature, which saves teammates critical seconds deciding whether to keep looting or flee.

### Quick heuristics

- **Large dot:** Hygrodere, Forest Keeper, Earth Leviathan, Eyeless Dog, or Old Bird.
- **Moves fast:** Bracken, Coil-Head, Hoarding Bug, or Snare Flea.
- **Moves erratically:** Bracken or Coil-Head.
- **Remains idle:** Bracken, Hoarding Bug, Snare Flea, Spore Lizard, or Bunker Spider.

### Indoor identification

- **Medium dot hovering near teammates without attacking** — Bracken stalking.
- **Medium-small dot that speeds in then suddenly stops** — Coil-Head.
- **Small dot speeding past teammates, ignoring them** — Hoarding Bug. A small dot in a loot-filled room is almost certainly one; a small dot moving alongside a scrap triangle is likely a Hoarding Bug, **though stiff movement suggests a Nutcracker carrying its shotgun**.
- **Medium dot, completely stationary** — Snare Flea.
- **Small dot, completely stationary** — Backwater Gunkfish.
- **Medium dot moving slowly** — Bunker Spider or Spore Lizard.
- **Large dot moving extremely sluggishly** — Hygrodere.
- **Medium dot moving in short rhythmic bursts** — Barber.
- **Medium dot that ignores teammates but lingers around one, or stops then rushes a lone teammate** — Butler.
- **Small dot steadily trailing players** — baby Maneater. They can be picked up, so a teammate overlapping the dot confirms it. **If that dot later becomes a large dot, it has matured into an adult Maneater.**
- **Teammate with a tiny red dot at their center** — infected with Cadaver Growths. **If the dot vanishes and they begin lunging at high speed at others, they have died and become a Cadaver Bloom.**
- **Erratic, unresponsive teammate** — possibly possessed by a Dramatic Mask.
- **Jester and Ghost Girl** cannot be identified on radar; the Ghost Girl is **completely undetected by radar cams** (use the CCTV instead).

### Outdoor identification

- **Colossal dot** — Earth Leviathan.
- **Very large dot with thumping** — Forest Keeper.
- **Large dot moving in rhythmic steps** — Old Bird (its light is another tell).
- **Medium-large dot with growling** — Eyeless Dog.
- **Small dot orbiting teammates** — Baboon Hawk.
- **Small dot hovering in one spot** — possibly a Kidnapper Fox; **if a teammate suddenly gets dragged toward it from a distance, it is certainly one.**
- **Large dot meandering and frequently stopping** — Giant Sapsucker. **If it suddenly accelerates at teammates regardless of range, it has aggroed.**
- **Tiny dot stalking from a distance and retreating when approached** — Feiopar.
- **Tiny dot** — Manticoil or Tulip Snake; if stationary, likely a Manticoil.
- **Circuit Bees** show no dot at all — they appear as a swarm over their hive, so it looks like a swarmed loot triangle.

## Ship Safety Warning

**Eyeless Dogs will follow noise into the ship.** Stay quiet, and remember that **furniture does not block them — only closing the doors does.** If you are using a Walkie-Talkie, **switch to text chat instead**: it does not attract Eyeless Dogs and teammates still receive the messages.

**Giant Sapsuckers can pry open the ship's doors**, so coordinate to avoid provoking one — most commonly by stealing one of its eggs.
